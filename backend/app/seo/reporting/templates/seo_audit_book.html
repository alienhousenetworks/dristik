[[chapter_ref:reading-guide]]
Reader Guide

How to Read This Report

This report translates SEO checks into business implications, evidence-backed findings, and practical next steps.

Every finding has two layers

Business explanation

What was observed, why it matters, and what the business owner should understand without needing technical knowledge.

Implementation detail

The affected URL, technical evidence, recommended change, verification method, and any limitations.

Evidence status definitions

StatusMeaning
Verified findingA specific issue was observed in the stated evidence.
Passed checkThe stated test ran and met its defined criteria.
Not testedThe check was not performed. This does not mean the website passed or failed.
UnavailableThe required source, permission, service, or measurement was unavailable.
SampledThe result applies to the inspected sample, not necessarily every page.
InferredA reasoned interpretation, not a directly observed fact.
Fixed — pending verificationA change was reported or applied, but the post-change check has not confirmed it.
Verified fixedThe same issue was retested after the change and the evidence supports resolution.
What this means for your business

A missing measurement is not proof of poor performance. The report will distinguish confirmed problems from areas where more data is needed before a reliable conclusion can be made.

  • The change has been implemented in the intended environment.

Important interpretation rules

  • Do not treat sampled URLs as a full-site inspection.
  • Do not treat unavailable metrics as zero.
  • Do not claim a ranking, traffic increase, lead increase or revenue outcome without supporting evidence.
  • Do not label a recommendation as implemented unless implementation evidence exists.
  • Do not label a change as verified fixed without a post-change test.
  • Do not present inferred causes as directly observed facts.
Final note for the reader
